\chapter{Conclusion}
\section{Summary of findings per research question}


\subsection{\hyperref[rq1]{\acs{RQ}1}: Segmentation quality}
The first research question concerned the non-interactive segmentation quality of \acs{SAM} compared to classical image processing baselines.
Measured with \acs{Dice} scores on a test set of 27 images, none of the tested \acs{SAM} variants outperformed the strongest classical baseline, \acs{Gaussian} thresholding.
On the contrary, the strongest \acs{SAM} variant performed \textit{significantly worse} - despite the inclusion of \acs{ICL} implementations and strategic \acs{POI} prompt placement.

\subsection{\hyperref[rq2]{\acs{RQ}2}: Interaction efficiency}
The second research question had to do with the efficiency of using \acs{SAM} as an interactive segmentation tool compared to a classical image processing baseline.
Measured in a case study of 3 documents from the test set with Ester Salgarella in terms of time spent in the tool and segmentation quality of the exports, the \acs{SAM}-based tool was beaten by the classical one both in terms of time and segmentation quality.

\subsection{\hyperref[rq3]{\acs{RQ}3}: Workflow efficiency}
The third research question concerned the time spent by Ester Salgarella to annotate manually compared to being assisted by the two self-developed tools was measured in a case study of 3 documents.

The \acs{SAM}2-based interactive tool did not show any potential of saving time in the workflow, both in total, and when only considering the post-editing phase.
Compared to the classical baselineworkflow of \acs{Gaussian} thresholding, it took more than twice the time to use.
And compared to the manual workflow, it took more than thrice the time to use.
\section{Overall contribution}
The results suggest that \acs{SAM} in the forms tested is not suitable for segmentation of Linear A tablets.
For each \acs{RQ}, \acs{SAM} failed compared to the baselines - even when guided by human interaction.
Not only did it not live up to its supposedly \acs{SOTA} superiority in terms of mere segmentation quality.
It also took more time to use, and did not save any time in the workflow whatsoever.
\section{Future Work}
\subsection{Alternative data sources}
Since the raw tablet based \acs{ML} system developed did not perform well enough to be deemed helpful for the workflow, another approach seems necessary in order to achieve a more automated workflow.
The bottleneck of the raw tablet based approach seems to be the quality of the input data, as the raw images are of low quality and contain a lot of noise, which is not only hard for the model to process, but also even for the researcher to differentiate the signs from mere tablet damage.
The suggestions generated should therefore instead be inferred from the corpus drawings, which are of higher quality, carry more strucured information than the raw tablet images, and are what the researcher already primarily uses as reference in the annotation process.
