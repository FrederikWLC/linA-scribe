\chapter{Conclusion}
\section{Summary of findings per research question}


\subsection{RQ1: Segmentation performance}
First, to answer the question, \textit{How well does a \acs{SAM}-based segmentation approach perform quantitatively in terms of segmentation accuracy, compared to simple classical image processing baselines when applied to Linear A document images?},
a non-interactive segmentation experiment was performed to compare the performance of \acs{SAM} to classical image processing baselines in terms of segmentation quality.

The tested \acs{SAM} variants on 27 raw tablet images did not outperform the best classical baseline, \acs{Gaussian} thresholding.
On the contrary, they all performed \textit{significantly worse} - even with inclusion of \acs{ICL} implementations and \acs{POI} prompt placement strategy.

\subsection{RQ2: Interaction efficiency}
Secondly, to answer the question, \textit{To which extent can user interaction be reduced by a \acs{SAM}-based semi-automatic segmentation workflow, while still maintaining high-quality outputs?},
two interactive segmentation tools - one based on \acs{SAM}2, one based on \acs{Gaussian} thresholding - were developed and evaluated on time spent in each tool by Ester Salgarella from image import to mask export and segmentation quality (i.e. \acs{Dice}) relative to ground truth (SigLA's Krita files), in a case study of 3 documents.

The \acs{SAM}2-based interactive tool did not show any potential of outperforming the \acs{Gaussian}-based one, as it both produced masks of lower quality (i.e. of lower \acs{Dice} scores), and took more time to use.

\subsection{RQ3: Workflow efficiency}
Finally, to answer the question, \textit{How much reduction in annotation time can be achieved using the proposed tool compared to fully manual digitization, under controlled experimental conditions?},
the time spent by Ester Salgarella to annotate manually compared to being assisted by the two self-developed tools was measured in a case study of 3 documents.

The \acs{SAM}2-based interactive tool did not show any potential of saving time in the workflow, both in total, and when only considering the post-editing phase.
Compared to the classical baselineworkflow of \acs{Gaussian} thresholding, it took more than twice the time to use.
And compared to the manual workflow, it took more than thrice the time to use.
\section{Overall contribution}
The results suggest that \acs{SAM} in the forms tested is not suitable for segmentation of Linear A tablets.
For each \acs{RQ}, \acs{SAM} failed compared to the baselines - even when guided by human interaction.
Not only did it not live up to its supposedly \acs{SOTA} superiority in terms of mere segmentation quality.
It also took more time to use, and did not save any time in the workflow whatsoever.
\section{Future Work}
\subsection{Alternative data sources}
Since the raw tablet based \acs{ML} system developed did not perform well enough to be deemed helpful for the workflow, another approach seems necessary in order to achieve a more automated workflow.
The bottleneck of the raw tablet based approach seems to be the quality of the input data, as the raw images are of low quality and contain a lot of noise, which is not only hard for the model to process, but also even for the researcher to differentiate the signs from mere tablet damage.
The suggestions generated should therefore instead be inferred from the corpus drawings, which are of higher quality, carry more strucured information than the raw tablet images, and are what the researcher already primarily uses as reference in the annotation process.
